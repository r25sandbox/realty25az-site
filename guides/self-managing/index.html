Thinking about self managing? A checklist before you fire your manager.

A fair look at the work, before you decide to handle the rental yourself or hire a property manager in North Scottsdale and nearby.

“Self managing works when you have the hours and the process.”

Before you take the file back.

Either path can be the right one. The checklist is the work you would own the day the manager is gone.

  1. 01

    Count the hours

    Showings, notices, vendor calls, and owner decisions land on weeknights and weekends. Write down who covers that time if you take it back.

  2. 02

    Know the rules you would carry

    Screening, ads, and the lease have to follow federal, state, and local fair housing laws. The person who screens is the person who answers if a step is skipped.

  3. 03

    Name the people you would call

    HVAC, plumbing, a locksmith, and someone who will show up for the rest. A list on paper is not the same as people who already know the house.

  4. 04

    Decide how rent gets in

    Due date, late notice, and where the money lands. You want a record of every dollar, not a text thread.

  5. 05

    Name who answers at 10pm

    A leak does not wait for business hours. If that person is you, say so before you fire the manager.

  6. 06

    Know where the file lives

    Lease, ledger, notices, and the move-in condition. If you cannot pull them in a few minutes, you are not ready to take the file over.

The same work, two ways

Neither path is a shortcut. Here is the work, side by side.

The workSelf managingHiring a manager
TimeYou cover showings, notices, and vendor calls, including evenings.The manager covers the day to day. You still approve expenses.
Fair housing and legal exposureYou are the one who must follow federal, state, and local fair housing laws on ads, screening, and the lease.The manager runs screening and the lease against those same laws. You still own the property.
Vendor benchYou find, check, and schedule each vendor.The manager uses people they already work with. You see the invoice.
Rent collectionYou track due dates, late notices, and deposits.The manager collects and sends you a record. You still see every dollar.
After-hours callsThe 10pm call comes to you.The call comes to the manager, who knows the vendors and the house.
RecordsYou keep the lease, the ledger, and the condition notes.The manager keeps the file and sends owner reports. Ask for copies before you leave.

Signs it's time to fire your manager

One slow week is not a pattern. These are.

  1. You cannot reach a person who knows the property.
  2. Repair bills carry a markup you did not agree to.
  3. You only hear from them when something is already late.
  4. Reports are missing, or you cannot see where the money went.
  5. You do not know who approved the last repair.

If those are not true, there may be nothing to fire. If they are, ask for an explanation in writing, then decide.
